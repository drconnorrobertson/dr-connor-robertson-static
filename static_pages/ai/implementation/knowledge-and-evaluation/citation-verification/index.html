Test Whether an AI Answer Is Supported by Its Citations

An AI answer can contain a genuine link and still make a claim the linked material does not support. The source may discuss a different service tier, a different date or a narrower condition. Checking that a link opens is therefore only one part of review. The more important question is whether the relevant passage supports the specific statement the answer asks a reader to believe.

This guide shows how to review that relationship using an original fictional service handbook and five illustrative claims. It does not report a measured model result, describe a real customer plan or certify an AI system. The example is deliberately small so the support decisions can be examined directly. Use the method to prepare a review appropriate to your actual workflow, with authorized sources and accountable decision owners.

The practical output is a claim-to-evidence sheet. It separates the wording of the claim, the source passage, the source version and the reviewer’s conclusion. A useful review can result in correction, qualification or removal. It should not turn a count of links into a reassuring accuracy score that hides missing evidence or an unresolved source conflict.

Begin with the answer and its intended use

Keep a copy of the answer being reviewed and identify its intended audience and use. A private brainstorming note and a customer-facing service commitment have different consequences. Determine what the reviewer is authorized and qualified to assess. Do not publish or act on an answer merely because someone has started a citation check; review status and release authority are separate questions.

Record the answer’s version and the question it was responding to. If a generated revision changes the wording, treat it as a new reviewable version rather than silently substituting it into the original sheet. The important relationship is between a particular statement and particular evidence. A review of an earlier sentence does not automatically cover a more expansive sentence produced later.

Keep unnecessary personal or confidential data out of the working sheet. Use the appropriate protected environment and only sources the reviewer may access. A citation can point to restricted material, and a public answer must not expose a passage simply because an internal reviewer can see it. Support, access rights and permission to disclose are distinct parts of the decision.

Split the answer into material factual claims

Break a sentence into separately reviewable claims when it contains more than one assertion. “All customers receive weekend support within two hours” combines eligibility, availability and response timing. A source might support one part and contradict another. Reviewing the whole sentence as a single unit can conceal the difference, especially when a generally correct clause makes the rest feel plausible.

Assign stable identifiers to the claims rather than using row numbers that change when the sheet is sorted. Preserve the answer’s actual wording, including words such as all, only, may and guaranteed. Those qualifiers can determine whether the statement is supported. Do not simplify the claim into a weaker proposition during review and then mark the original stronger wording as accepted.

Decide which claims matter to the intended use. This guide does not prescribe a universal sampling rule. If a statement can influence a meaningful customer, operational or professional decision, its evidence needs appropriate scrutiny. A reviewed subset should remain a reviewed subset; do not label an entire long answer verified when only a few convenient sentences were checked.

Identify the evidence actually being cited

Open the cited source through an authorized route and locate the relevant passage. A document title, search snippet or citation marker is not itself the supporting evidence. Check that the source exists and that it is the source the answer describes. If the link is broken or the cited passage cannot be found, keep that issue visible rather than reconstructing a plausible passage from memory.

Record enough location information to let another authorized reviewer find the passage, such as a section heading or page reference appropriate to the document. Preserve surrounding context where necessary. A single sentence can be misleading when a nearby paragraph limits its application. The review should compare meaning in context, not just matching keywords or a similar phrase.

Do not let the model’s explanation of why a citation supports a claim substitute for opening the source. NIST’s Generative AI Profile discusses confabulation, including unreliable generated explanations or citations. That primary risk context supports independent checking; it does not validate this worksheet or guarantee that a human reviewer will catch every error. Keep the actual passage available for inspection.

Check source authority and version separately

A passage can support a statement about what a document says without establishing that the document is current or authoritative for the workflow. Identify who owns the source, which version applies and whether it is approved for the intended use. A draft handbook and an approved service policy can contain similar wording while having very different operational status.

Record effective dates and any relevant review status. If a source describes a previous service arrangement, an answer about current coverage may require different evidence. Do not infer that the newest-looking file name is authoritative. Use the organization’s actual source-control and approval process. Where the applicable source is unclear, escalate that question rather than selecting the version that makes the generated answer look correct.

Keep source suitability distinct from claim support in the sheet. A claim may faithfully summarize an obsolete document while still being unsuitable for release as current guidance. Conversely, a current approved document may fail to support the particular sentence. A single green status should not collapse these separate questions into a conclusion no one actually reviewed.

Read the fictional approved handbook carefully

The fictional handbook says that premium-plan customers may request weekend support, that requests enter the ordinary support queue and that the handbook states no guaranteed response interval. It also says a customer-specific agreement must be checked for additional terms. These statements are invented for this exercise. They do not describe any actual service offered by Dr Connor Robertson, Elixir or another organization.

The first illustrative answer claims that every customer receives weekend support. That conflicts with the fictional plan restriction. The second says premium customers may request weekend support, which matches the stated eligibility under the supplied passage. The third promises a two-hour response, which the passage does not establish. A correct handbook link beside any of these sentences would not make the unsupported wording acceptable.

The fourth claim says weekend support is available but omits the premium restriction, making the wording incomplete for an audience that includes other plans. The fifth says premium customers may request weekend support subject to their applicable agreement, preserving the supplied qualification. The worksheet records the reviewer’s teaching judgments; it is not an automated or observed model evaluation.

Use support labels with defined meanings

For this exercise, full support means the cited passage supports the material claim as worded within the stated context. Partial support means some material part is supported while another part or qualification needs attention. Absent support means the supplied passage does not establish the claim. Contradicted means the passage conflicts with the claim. These are working definitions, not an external certification scheme.

Apply the definitions consistently and preserve the reason for each label. A label without explanation can be difficult to review or correct. If the claim is ambiguous, clarify the wording or mark the uncertainty instead of forcing a confident category. A reviewer may need another source or subject-matter judgment before deciding whether the answer is fit for its intended use.

In the five fictional rows, the teaching labels are two full, one partial, one absent and one contradicted. That is two fully supported claims out of five reviewed, or forty percent within this invented set. It is not the accuracy rate of a model, a probability that a new answer is correct or a measure of the reliability of an entire production system.

Preserve qualifiers when proposing a correction

A corrected sentence should remain within the evidence. Replacing every customer with premium customers addresses one eligibility problem, but it does not justify adding a response-time promise. Compare the revised claim with the source again. A correction is not complete merely because it removes the most obvious error; another unsupported detail can remain or be introduced during the edit.

Use the source’s actual level of certainty. If it says a customer may request a service, do not rewrite that as a guarantee that the service will be delivered under every circumstance. Preserve relevant restrictions, exceptions and effective dates. A shorter answer can be useful, but brevity is not a reason to erase a condition that materially changes what readers can expect.

If the source does not answer the question, say what is unresolved and identify the appropriate next step. Do not fill the gap with a likely industry practice or a plausible number. The right output may be a narrower answer or a request to consult the applicable agreement. A citation review should help the answer abstain where the evidence stops.

Keep quotation checking distinct from meaning checking

If the answer presents text as a direct quotation, verify the quoted wording against the actual source. Check omissions and surrounding context. A faithful quotation can still be used to imply a broader conclusion than the passage supports, so exact matching does not complete the review. Conversely, a paraphrase can be supported without repeating the source word for word.

Avoid turning a source-checking sheet into an unnecessary copy of a long protected document. Retain the minimum authorized excerpt or reference needed for the review under the relevant handling rules. The fictional handbook in this guide is original teaching material, so it can be included without exposing an actual organization’s policy or customer record.

When a quotation is inaccurate, correct it through the appropriate editorial process and review the resulting claim. Do not merely remove quotation marks while leaving a misleading paraphrase. The distinction matters because readers may place extra confidence in text that appears to be quoted exactly. Preserve both the source’s words and its intended scope.

Handle conflicting sources without averaging them

If two apparently relevant sources disagree, identify the conflict and investigate authority, version and context. One may be a draft, apply to a different plan or describe an earlier period. Do not combine them into a compromise statement simply because both are cited. A sentence supported by neither source is not improved by attaching two links.

Record which question needs an owner’s decision. For example, the team may need to determine which approved policy applies to a particular service arrangement. The model should not resolve that governance question by choosing the more convenient document. Keep the answer on hold or narrow its scope until the responsible review establishes what can properly be stated.

A conflict record should be specific enough for another person to investigate. Identify the relevant passages and the material difference, while avoiding unnecessary confidential detail. Do not describe a disagreement as resolved merely because a summary mentions both positions. Acknowledging a conflict and resolving it are different states, with different implications for release.

Review retrieval failures as a separate problem

Sometimes the answer’s claim is unsupported because the relevant document was not retrieved, even though an authorized source exists elsewhere. That possibility does not make the current answer supported. Mark the evidence gap in the answer under review, then investigate the retrieval or source-selection issue separately. Keep the distinction between fixing the workflow and accepting the existing output.

Test retrieval with questions appropriate to the approved source set and access rules. Do not broaden access just to make a citation appear. A user who is not permitted to see a document should not receive its protected content through an answer. The separate retrieval-permissions guide addresses that operating question; the claim sheet does not grant additional access rights.

If the workflow is revised, review new outputs against the appropriate evidence. A repaired search configuration does not retroactively validate earlier answers. Preserve the relationship between the output version, source set and review result. This traceability helps the team understand whether a later improvement addresses the actual failure or merely produces a different-looking citation list.

Treat automated checks as aids with limits

Software can help flag a missing source field, a repeated identifier or an unreachable link. Those checks concern structure or availability. They do not necessarily establish that a passage entails the claim. A semantic support decision requires a method appropriate to the actual use and consequences. Do not label a formatting validator a factual verifier or use a link count as a substitute for evidence review.

An additional model can also generate a critique, but that critique needs its own interpretation and may contain errors. Keep any automated suggestion separate from the accountable review decision. If the workflow relies on automated support judgments, evaluate them against a suitable reviewed set and document their limits. This guide does not supply a tested classifier or claim a particular model can perform that task reliably.

The downloadable sheet contains prewritten fictional judgments for discussion, not executable verification logic. Its value is that every claim and passage can be inspected. If someone adapts it into software, the input contract, evaluation design, handling of uncertainty and release authority need deliberate work. Adding automation should not erase the evidence boundary that made the small example understandable.

Record the disposition rather than just the score

For each material issue, state whether the claim was corrected, qualified, removed or left unresolved. Identify what evidence supports the revised disposition. A team needs to know what happened to the unsupported two-hour promise, not merely that the answer’s score improved after editing. Keep the actual final wording available so the next reviewer can assess the released statement.

Distinguish a review recommendation from release approval. A content reviewer may propose a correction without having authority to publish a service commitment. Follow the actual approval process for the intended output. A completed row in a worksheet cannot create contractual authority, professional sign-off or permission to disclose information that the organization has not granted.

When the answer is revised again, identify which claims need another review. An unchanged supported claim may retain its traceable evidence, while new or materially altered wording requires attention. Avoid repeatedly checking the same unaltered link merely to produce activity. Focus the review on changed meaning, source changes and unresolved risks relevant to the actual workflow.

Make the final explanation useful to the recipient

A released answer should explain the supported information in clear language and retain material restrictions. It need not expose the entire internal review sheet to every reader. Provide citations that the intended recipient can use appropriately, and avoid linking to protected resources in a way that reveals information they should not receive. Audience and access remain part of the editorial decision.

For the fictional handbook question, a bounded answer would identify premium-plan eligibility and direct the reader to the applicable agreement for additional terms, without inventing a response interval. That is a teaching example, not actual service advice. A real answer must use the actual approved source and any relevant customer-specific conditions under the organization’s process.

The useful conclusion is proportional to the evidence: these claims were checked against these passages under these definitions, with these remaining limits. That statement is less dramatic than declaring an answer fully trustworthy, but it gives a responsible team something concrete to assess. Citation verification works best when it preserves uncertainty rather than decorating it with links.

Keep the denominator explicit in a review summary

If a summary reports the number of supported claims, state how many claims were selected and how they were selected. Five reviewed claims from one invented answer are not five independent customer cases. A partial-support label also should not silently count as full support. Preserve the category totals and the selection limits so another reader can reconstruct the result without guessing what the percentage includes.

Frequently asked questions

Is a real source link enough to verify an AI answer?

No. A link can exist while its passage supports a different claim, plan, date or context. Locate the relevant text and compare it with the actual wording, including material qualifiers. Also check source authority and suitability for the intended use. Link availability is one check; it is not proof that the answer’s factual claims are supported.

What should I do with a partly supported sentence?

Identify which part is supported and which part needs correction or qualification. Split combined claims where useful and review the revised wording against the source again. Do not mark the original broad sentence accepted because a weaker paraphrase would be true. Keep unresolved details visible and use an appropriate owner when the evidence does not settle them.

Does the fictional forty-percent result measure a model?

No. Two of five prewritten teaching claims are labeled fully supported under the example’s definitions. The claims and judgments are invented, not observed model outputs or a representative evaluation set. The fraction does not establish production accuracy, general reliability or a probability that another answer is correct. It only describes the supplied fictional rows.

Can another AI system do the evidence review for me?

An automated critique can help identify questions, but it can also make errors and needs evaluation appropriate to the workflow. Keep suggestions distinct from accountable decisions. This guide does not validate a model-based verifier. Use suitable reviewed examples, uncertainty handling and release controls before relying on automated support judgments for consequential outputs.

What if the cited sources disagree?

Record the specific conflict and investigate version, authority and scope. Do not average incompatible passages into a statement neither supports. Ask the responsible source owner or reviewer to resolve the applicable policy question. Until then, narrow the answer or keep it unresolved through the appropriate process. Mentioning both documents does not itself resolve their disagreement.

When should a corrected answer be checked again?

Review materially changed wording, new claims and relevant source changes before relying on the revised answer. A prior check applies to the output and evidence actually reviewed. Preserve the final wording and disposition so another reviewer can trace what was accepted, corrected or removed. A change to retrieval or formatting does not automatically validate every earlier claim.
