A red flag is a reason to investigate a claim, not a substitute for due diligence. The useful question is what evidence would confirm the issue, who can assess it, and which decision must wait for that assessment. This guide turns warning signs into a practical issue register.

The historical article at this URL discussed common weaknesses in small business deals. This updated edition focuses on how to document observations without treating a seller’s explanation, an AI summary, or an attractive presentation as verified evidence.

Separate observations from conclusions

Write the observation in a form another reviewer can reproduce: two supplied records show different totals for the same period; a customer schedule lacks contract references; a process has no identified backup. Avoid converting those observations immediately into accusations or final judgments about value.

Keep four fields separate: the claim, the evidence received, the unresolved question, and the decision affected. An issue can remain open even after a seller responds. It closes when the responsible reviewer accepts sufficient evidence and records the conclusion.

Start with these evidence questions

  • Owner dependence: Which customer, supplier, approval, and technical relationships require the seller personally? Request a responsibility map and test the proposed handoff.
  • Customer concentration: Which records support customer-level revenue and the continuity of those relationships? Have the finance and commercial leads verify the data and assumptions.
  • Financial inconsistencies: Which statements, schedules, and transaction records fail to reconcile? Ask the qualified financial reviewer to identify what remains unresolved.
  • Unsupported adjustments: What evidence supports each proposed earnings adjustment, and what continuing cost would replace the removed expense?
  • Operational gaps: Which work depends on undocumented judgment, overdue maintenance, or a single unavailable employee?
  • Contract and permission gaps: Which important rights, relationships, or operating permissions require professional review before a transition?

These are starting questions rather than a complete diligence list. The Small Business Administration’s business planning resources provide background on buying an existing business and assembling the relevant information.

Build an issue register with a decision attached

Use one row per issue: issue ID, observation, source and version, business consequence to investigate, evidence request, reviewer, due date, current status, and acceptance condition. If the consequence is unknown, record that uncertainty. A short register with clear ownership is more useful than a long warning list with no next step.

Keep the register separate from the full acquisition diligence checklist. The checklist tracks coverage; the register tracks unresolved findings arising from that coverage.

Worked example: a recurring-revenue claim

Illustrative example: a presentation describes customer revenue as recurring. The contract schedule includes both renewable agreements and orders placed without continuing commitments. Record the distinction, link the supplied sources, and ask the commercial and legal reviewers to verify the appropriate classification. Do not change the figures to match the presentation or assume every repeat order is contractually committed.

Use explicit closure conditions

  1. Assign the issue to the person qualified to assess it.
  2. Request the specific missing record or explanation.
  3. Preserve the original observation when new evidence arrives.
  4. Record the reviewer’s conclusion, remaining uncertainty, and which decision may now proceed.

Some findings may require more investigation, a transition condition, a revised operating plan, or a decision to stop. Those choices depend on the transaction and the responsible advisers. This article does not set a purchase-price adjustment or determine whether a deal should close.

Carry unresolved findings into the transition

When an accepted operating issue remains for the post-close team, retain the evidence, the agreed action, its owner, and the due trigger. Do not let an issue disappear because a deal-room folder has been archived. Use the acquisition team AI implementation plan to organize the handoff, with people verifying the final record.
